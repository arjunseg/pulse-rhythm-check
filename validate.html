Signal-Processing Validation

Synthetic pulse signals with known heartbeat times are pushed through the exact same dsp.js pipeline the camera app uses (analyzeRecording()), and the output is compared with the known truth.

Scope: this validates the signal-processing pipeline only. It makes no claim about clinical accuracy. The app has not been validated against ECG and is not a medical device.

Summary

How the test signals are built

Each recording is 45 s long. Beat times are generated first (the ground truth). A pulse shape is placed at every beat time: a sharp rise to the main (systolic) peak, a slower fall, and a small second bump about 330 ms later (the dicrotic wave). The pulse is subtracted from a steady brightness of 180, because in the camera image the brightness dips with each beat. Random Gaussian sensor noise is added. Samples are taken at about 30 frames per second with random timing jitter, like a real phone camera.

Pipeline settings under test (from dsp.js)

Scoring: a detected beat counts as matching a true beat if it lies within ±150 ms of it. Only true beats between 1 s and 44 s are scored, so beats cut off by the start or end of the recording don't count against the pipeline. Interval error is measured for every pair of consecutive true beats that were both matched.
